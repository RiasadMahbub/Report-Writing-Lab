# Style Guide — IMF Reports

IMF publishes in (at least) three distinct registers. Don't blend them.

## 1. Corporate operations reports (Environmental Sustainability Reports)

- Leadership letter opens the report, signed by a named director, framing the
  year's context (e.g. COVID recovery) before any data appears.
- Chart-first pages: one chart or table per page/topic, minimal prose around it.
- Emissions reported by GHG Protocol Scope 1/2/3, always with a year-over-year
  or multi-year comparison chart.
- Carbon offsets reported as a literal ledger table: country | tons | # projects | project type.
- Sentence pattern: short, declarative, causal — "This was mostly a result of
  Fund staff gradually returning to their offices."
- Section labels are plain nouns, not gerunds (contrast with World Bank):
  "Waste and Water Management," "Commuting and Transportation," "Other Initiatives."

Template:
```
[TOPIC IN CAPS AS HEADER]

[1–2 sentence description of what changed this year.] [Cause of the change,
named plainly.] [Number: metric ton / percent / dollar figure with baseline year.]
```

## 2. Staff Climate Notes / research notes

- Abstract has an explicit numbered contribution list: "(i) it proposes...,
  (ii) it conducts empirical analysis, finding that..., (iii) it discusses..."
- Every empirical claim in the abstract already carries its number:
  "nearly 38 percent of bank loans... are to harmful subsidies-dependent sectors."
- Structure: Abstract → Introduction/motivation → Conceptual framework →
  Empirical analysis → Findings → Policy implications.
- Vocabulary assumes expertise: "double materiality," "transmission channels,"
  "Minsky moment" — no glossary, no hand-holding.
- Passive voice tolerated more than in corporate reports ("is degrading nature
  at an unprecedented pace").

## 3. Flagship reports (GFSR, WEO, Fiscal Monitor)

- Chaptered like a book; each chapter can stand alone.
- Executive summary up front translates technical findings into 3–5 plain
  takeaways for a policymaker audience.
- Heavy hedging language for forward-looking claims: "risks are tilted to the
  downside," "scenarios suggest."

## Cross-cutting IMF habits

- "The Fund" is used instead of "IMF" or "we" in formal prose — a house-style tell.
- Fiscal year anchors ("FY22") appear constantly, sometimes alongside calendar year.
- Numbers are almost never rounded to vague terms — always a specific percent
  or unit, with the comparison baseline stated in the same sentence.
