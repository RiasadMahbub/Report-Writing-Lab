# Cross-Institution Comparison

Use this table to figure out which genre you're writing in, then jump to the
matching style guide and template.

| Feature | World Bank ESG report | IMF corporate report | IMF research note | WEF flagship | Academic paper / think-tank brief |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core unit | Values-based section (gerund headers) | Plain topic header | Numbered contribution list | Chapter + appendix methodology | Abstract → Method → Findings |
| Primary evidence | Internal GHG/HR/procurement data | Internal GHG/facilities data | Original empirical analysis | Expert perception survey | Literature + own analysis |
| Voice | "We are committed to..." | "The Fund..." | Passive/technical, no "we" | "The world in 2025..." | "This paper argues/finds..." |
| Number style | % + $ + baseline year, GRI-coded | Metric tons, YoY comparison | Precise %, cited to a specific dataset | Survey-derived rankings/scores | Regression coefficients, effect sizes |
| Tone | Aspirational, self-reporting | Factual, operational | Analytical, hedged | Narrative, forecasting | Argumentative, evidence-led |
| Best template | `corporate-esg-section-template.md` | `corporate-esg-section-template.md` (IMF variant notes) | `research-note-template.md` | `research-note-template.md` (survey variant) | `policy-brief-template.md` |

## Quick decision guide

- Writing about **your own organization's footprint or operations** → corporate ESG template.
- Writing an **original empirical finding** (something you analyzed yourself) → research note template.
- Writing to **influence a policy decision or critique a program** → policy brief template.
- Writing a **forecast or landscape overview based on expert input** → WEF-style survey variant of the research note template.

## The single biggest style tell across all of them

Every one of these institutions **quantifies every claim**. If a sentence in
your draft doesn't have a number, a source, or a named mechanism attached to
it, it will read as amateur next to real institutional writing — that's the
fastest fix to make a practice piece sound credible.
