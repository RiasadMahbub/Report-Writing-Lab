# Template — Staff Research Note / Working Paper

Use for: writing in the voice of an IMF Staff Climate Note, a WEF-style
survey-based analysis, or an academic working paper.

```markdown
# [Title: descriptive, not clever — states the topic plainly]

## Abstract

[1 sentence: the broad problem/context.] [1 sentence: why current knowledge
is insufficient.] This note/paper contributes in [N] ways: (i) [conceptual
contribution — a framework, typology, or model]; (ii) [empirical
contribution — what you analyzed and the headline finding, WITH A NUMBER];
(iii) [policy contribution — what this implies for decision-makers].

## 1. Introduction / Motivation

[Why this question, why now. Cite 2–3 pieces of existing literature or
institutional context the reader needs.]

## 2. Conceptual Framework / Methodology

[Name your method in one plain-language sentence.] [Describe data sources,
sample, and any assumptions.] [State limitations up front, briefly.]

## 3. Findings

[Each finding gets its own short paragraph, quantified, with the source of
the number stated inline.]

## 4. Policy Implications / Discussion

[What should a policymaker, regulator, or institution do differently given
these findings? Be specific — a lever, a threshold, a monitoring metric.]

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

[One honest paragraph — what would change your conclusion, what you didn't
have data for.]
```

## Checklist before you call it done

- [ ] Abstract has a numbered (i)/(ii)/(iii) contribution list.
- [ ] Every finding has a specific number attached, with its source.
- [ ] Method is named in a sentence a non-specialist could repeat back.
- [ ] At least one limitation is stated explicitly, not buried.
- [ ] Ends with a specific, actionable policy implication — not "more
      research is needed."
